Should an airstrip carry cargo? Lodwar, Turkana

Most freight in Kenya moves by road, and for most goods that is exactly right. So when does an airstrip make a difference? This week we put Lodwar to the Lift Test: seven questions, a draft score from public information, and the conditions that would make cargo work.

What we found

Kenya Airports Authority has rehabilitated Lodwar Airport, with a terminal designed for around 80 passengers: departure and arrival lounges, security screening and an administration block. There is passenger service, and UN and NGO flights run there too.

A new road from Kitale has shortened the journey by road, and truck operators are reported to be making more trips. That is good news for Turkana. It also means trucks can carry the bulk, so the air route has to earn its place by carrying what cannot wait for a truck: medicines, lab samples, spare parts, and perhaps fresh fish. Trucks and planes are partners.

The question that decides most cases is not the runway. It is whether there is a named buyer with steady volume: an anchor. We could not find one in public information. That is the open question, and an invitation for the people who know Turkana best to tell us more.

The conditions that make cargo work at Lodwar

  • 1 A named anchor buyer with steady volume
  • 2 A small cargo-capable aircraft on a regular schedule
  • 3 Passengers sharing the cost of the schedule
  • 4 A cold chain at the lake first, so fish has its own early links
  • 5 The new road treated as a partner, not a competitor
