The Lift Test: Methodology v1.0

How we judge whether cargo could pay its way at an East African airstrip. Open method, public sources, corrections welcome.

Why this exists

Most freight in East Africa moves by road, and for most goods that is the right answer. Air freight pays only when goods are worth a lot per kilogram or cannot wait. The Lift Test asks seven questions to find where an airstrip makes that difference, and what would have to be true for cargo to work. A low score is not a failure: many strips do something more important than cargo, such as keeping people connected and safe.

The seven questions (score each 0, 1 or 2)

Question0 (weak)1 (conditional)2 (strong)
1 Value densityBulk or low-value goods onlySome high-value nichesClear high-value flow
2 Time sensitivityGoods can waitSome goods cannot waitMost targeted goods lose value fast
3 Road gapRoad is fast and reliableMeaningful but narrowing gapDays saved, or road often cut
4 Anchor demandNone identifiedPossible anchor, volume unknownNamed anchor, regular volume
5 BackhaulOne direction onlyImbalancedBalanced two-way flow
6 Passenger baseNo scheduled serviceOccasional or new serviceEstablished regular service
7 Aircraft fitLimits most aircraftSmall aircraft onlyComfortable for likely aircraft

Bands

11 to 14Cargo-led
7 to 10Conditional
0 to 6People-first

None of the bands is a failure. A strip that does a different job well is doing its job.

Rules we keep

  • Every score is published with its conditions: what would have to be true.
  • Public sources only. We cite them in the caption.
  • Validation partners (authorities, operators) check facts, not conclusions or scores.
  • Sponsors, if any, never see scores before publication or influence them. Commercial relationships are disclosed on the content.
  • Corrections are welcome and are logged below, with the date.
